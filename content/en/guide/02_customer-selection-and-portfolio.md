## From territory to portfolio

In the traditional approach a rep gets a piece of the map: “Everyone in this district is yours.” The outcome is familiar: the rep visits the customers they know and like; high-potential but difficult customers are neglected; hundreds of customers go years without a single visit.

In the **portfolio approach** the rep gets a manageable number of named customers, each selected for potential and fit. The rest are served through catalogues, phone or digital channels.

<div class="grid2">
<div class="box"><h4>Territory approach</h4><ul><li>Hundreds of customers per rep</li><li>Visits driven by habit</li><li>Shallow relationships, irregular frequency</li><li>Success depends on the person</li></ul></div>
<div class="box dark"><h4>Portfolio approach</h4><ul style="color:#C9D1E0"><li>A selected, manageable number of customers</li><li>Frequency driven by potential</li><li>Share of wallet and category use tracked</li><li>Success depends on the system</li></ul></div>
</div>

<div class="callout warn"><span class="t">A hard truth</span><p>Giving a rep more customers than they can manage is not a management decision; it is avoiding one. Inflating a list is easy; calculating the right number and then demanding results is hard.</p></div>

## Eight criteria for portfolio selection

| # | Criterion | The question it asks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Current sales volume | How much do they buy from us today? |
| 2 | Purchase potential | How much do they buy in total (from us and competitors)? |
| 3 | Share of wallet | What percentage of their total spend is with us? |
| 4 | Purchase frequency | How often do they order? |
| 5 | Category penetration | In how many categories do they work with us? |
| 6 | Profitability | Does selling to this customer leave a profit? |
| 7 | Strategic importance | Reference value, chain, location or trend effect? |
| 8 | Operational fit | Do they fit our delivery, payment and service model? |

The most valuable insight is usually **the gap between potential and current sales**. High potential, low share: growth opportunity. High share, low potential: protect and serve efficiently. Low potential, low share: does not justify the cost of one-to-one selling.

| Segment | Profile | Management recipe |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | High potential, high or growable share | Weekly contact, annual customer plan, also known to the manager |
| **B** | Medium-high potential, low share | Fortnightly visit, category expansion, winning share from competitors |
| **C** | Medium potential, standard needs | Monthly visit, promotion and order follow-up |
| **Passive / at risk** | Low potential, irregular buying or payment risk | Phone, digital, catalogue; no one-to-one visits |

## Hunters and farmers: two different muscles

<div class="grid2">
<div class="box"><h4>Hunter</h4><p><strong>Focus:</strong> identifying new customers, opening doors, first orders.<br><strong>Competencies:</strong> high energy, resilience to rejection, quick rapport.<br><strong>KPI:</strong> new customers placing a second order; active rate after 90 days.</p></div>
<div class="box"><h4>Farmer</h4><p><strong>Focus:</strong> loyalty, category expansion, share of wallet.<br><strong>Competencies:</strong> analytical thinking, patience, consultative style.<br><strong>KPI:</strong> penetration, share-of-wallet growth, profitability, customer churn.</p></div>
</div>

When a hunter’s new customer reaches a defined maturity (e.g. three months of regular buying), it moves to a farmer through a written **handover** process. Without one, the customer gets lost between two reps.

## Actions by customer lifecycle stage

| Stage | Situation | Main action |
|---|---|---|
| Prospect | Not yet a customer | Get to know, discover needs, first offer |
| New | First order placed | Secure the second order, monitor the experience |
| Developing | Buys regularly, low share | Expand categories, cross-sell |
| Stable | High share | Protect, serve efficiently, reward loyalty |
| Declining | Purchases falling | Find the cause (price, service, competitor, business decline), recovery plan |
| Passive | No purchases for a long time | Win-back offer or remove from portfolio |

## How to size a portfolio

1. **Monthly visit capacity** = field days × quality visits per day. Example: 17 × 6 = 102 visits.
2. **Average monthly visits per customer** = segment frequencies weighted by portfolio mix. Example: 20% A (4), 35% B (2), 45% C (1) → 2.0.
3. **Portfolio size** ≈ 102 ÷ 2.0 ≈ 51 customers. Leaving 10–15% for prospecting and collections gives 44–51.

## Portfolio changes: who comes in, who goes out?

- **In:** a customer whose potential has been estimated, segment assigned and operational fit confirmed.
- **Out:** a customer well below target for two periods, carrying payment risk or not covering the cost of one-to-one selling.
- **Rule:** changes are made periodically (e.g. quarterly), with a written reason and manager approval. Random moves undermine target and bonus fairness.

## Implementation flow

<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Clean the customer data.</strong> Remove closed, duplicate and incomplete records.</li>
<li><strong>Estimate potential</strong> using indicators such as seats, size, menu or number of outlets.</li>
<li><strong>Analyse current performance:</strong> sales, frequency, categories, profitability.</li>
<li><strong>Assign segments</strong> and management recipes.</li>
<li><strong>Split hunters and farmers</strong> and write the handover rules.</li>
<li><strong>Divide portfolios by capacity</strong> and flag them in the system.</li>
<li><strong>Review monthly:</strong> segment moves, customers lost and won.</li>
</ol>

## Common mistakes

- Selecting the portfolio only by past sales (ignoring potential).
- Giving every rep the same number of customers despite different segment mixes.
- Keeping passive customers “in case they buy”.
- Squeezing new customer acquisition in between existing portfolio work.

## Manager check questions

<ul class="check">
<li>Is each rep’s portfolio size consistent with their calculated capacity?</li>
<li>Is customer potential recorded in the system, or only sales?</li>
<li>Is there a written plan for every A-segment customer?</li>
<li>Are the hunter-to-farmer handover rules written down?</li>
<li>How many customers entered and left portfolios last quarter, and why?</li>
</ul>
