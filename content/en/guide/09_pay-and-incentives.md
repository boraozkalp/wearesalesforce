## A bad bonus scheme produces bad behaviour

Sales teams do what they are paid for. A scheme that rewards only sales increases discounting, erodes margin and encourages selling to risky customers. A scheme that balances sales, profit, customer development and collections produces sustainable growth.

<div class="grid2">
<div class="box"><h4>The dangerous equation</h4><p>Sales-only bonus + uncontrolled discounts = margin loss + wrong customers → sales go up, company profit goes down.</p></div>
<div class="box dark"><h4>The sustainable equation</h4><p>Sales + profit + share of wallet + collections + behaviour quality = healthy growth + the right customer relationships.</p></div>
</div>

## Components of pay

| Component | Purpose | Typical share |
|---|---|---|
| Base salary | Security, loyalty, reward for competence | 70–85% of total pay |
| Bonus (variable) | Rewarding target achievement | 15–30% of total pay |
| Incentives | Short-term focus (campaigns, contests) | Small and occasional |
| Benefits | Car, phone, meals, health, training | According to market |

A higher fixed share (especially for farmers) protects long-term customer relationships; hunters can have a somewhat higher variable share.

### Pay by competency level

Linking base pay to competency level (beginner, developing, competent, expert) rather than seniority alone rewards development and makes pay bands transparent. Annual increases within the band depend on performance and market conditions.

## Features of a good bonus scheme

<ul class="check">
<li><strong>Simple:</strong> reps can calculate their own bonus.</li>
<li><strong>Fair:</strong> it accounts for differences in portfolio potential.</li>
<li><strong>Effective:</strong> few KPIs aligned with strategy (no more than 4).</li>
<li><strong>Timely:</strong> short time between result and payment.</li>
<li><strong>Predictable:</strong> threshold and cap keep the budget under control.</li>
<li><strong>Controlled:</strong> calculations verified by two separate people (four-eyes principle).</li>
</ul>

## Which KPIs in the bonus?

| KPI | Why | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| Sales | Basic growth indicator | Never on its own |
| Gross profit | Discount discipline | Needs data infrastructure |
| Active customers / penetration | Breadth of the customer base | Define clearly (e.g. at least 1 purchase a month) |
| Share of wallet | Depth with each customer | Needs potential estimates |
| New customers (second order) | For hunters | Quality, not count |
| Collections / overdue | Financial health | Must be within the rep’s influence |
| Coaching / behaviour score | Quality of method | Keep weight low, avoid subjectivity |

- **Threshold** (e.g. 80%): no bonus below it; protects the baseline expectation.
- **Target** (100%): full target bonus.
- **Accelerator:** higher payout for every point above target, rewarding outstanding performance.
- **Cap** (e.g. 130%): protects the budget and limits distortions from unrealistic targets or one-off large orders.

## Bonus design by role (example weights)

| Role | Sales | Gross profit | Customer development | New customers | Collections |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Account Manager (farmer) | 35% | 25% | 25% | – | 15% |
| New Business (hunter) | 20% | 15% | – | 50% | 15% |
| Key Account Manager | 35% | 35% | 15% | – | 15% |
| Field Sales Manager | Team KPIs 70% + coaching and team development 30% | | | | |

## Team or individual bonus?

Individual bonuses clarify accountability; team bonuses encourage cooperation and knowledge sharing. A practical balance: mostly individual (70–80%) with a smaller team or regional component (20–30%).

## Discount discipline

Discounting is a sales tool, not a sales strategy. Define authority levels for each discount band (Module 7), ask for something in return (volume, payment terms, a new category) and include gross profit in the bonus.

## Incentives and contests

Short-term incentives (e.g. launching a new category) create focus, but if they become permanent the team starts waiting for them before doing normal work. Keep them rare, time-boxed and measurable, and don’t neglect non-monetary recognition (visibility, training opportunities, sharing within the team).

## Common mistakes

1. Paying bonus on sales only. 2. Too many KPIs. 3. Unrealistic targets. 4. Non-transparent calculation. 5. Ignoring portfolio differences. 6. Not measuring new customer quality. 7. Not measuring profitability. 8. Leaving out collections. 9. Not rewarding data and follow-up discipline. 10. Not reviewing the scheme annually.

## Manager check questions

<ul class="check">
<li>Can my reps calculate their own bonus?</li>
<li>Does our scheme include profitability and collections?</li>
<li>Does the distribution of payouts reflect real performance differences?</li>
<li>Have we reviewed the scheme in the last 12 months?</li>
</ul>
