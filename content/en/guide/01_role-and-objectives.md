## Why a separate management discipline?

In many companies the sales team is seen as people who “go out, collect orders and present promotions”. Success then depends on individual effort and relationships. When a rep leaves, customer knowledge leaves too; two reps quote different prices to the same customer; the manager cannot tell what actually works.

A systematic sales force answers the same questions the same way every day:

- Which customer do we visit, how often and for what purpose?
- What do we expect each visit to change for the customer?
- How do we measure both the result and the behaviour that produced it?
- How do we close the gaps we measure through coaching and training?

> Sales force management is the work of moving sales from personal effort to systematic performance.

## Vision: the partner who grows the customer’s business

The vision of a good sales team is not “selling products” but **being a trusted business partner who helps customers grow their own business**. Whether the customer is a restaurant, hotel, café, shop or distributor, what they want is better margins, less stock hassle, reliable supply and solutions that delight their own customers.

This changes three things:

1. **The topic of conversation** shifts from price and discount to the customer’s gain.
2. **The length of the relationship** extends from one order to years.
3. **The yardstick** becomes not just sales, but the share we win of the customer’s total spend (share of wallet).

## Mission: five core tasks

<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Focus on the right customers.</strong> Spend time on customers with the highest potential and the best fit with the company’s model.</li>
<li><strong>Help customers grow their business.</strong> Propose products in the context of their menu, shelf and costs.</li>
<li><strong>Grow share per customer.</strong> Increase our share of each customer’s total purchases, category by category.</li>
<li><strong>Build accurate customer data.</strong> Record potential, buying habits, decision-makers and competitor information systematically.</li>
<li><strong>Work to a plan and measurably.</strong> Visits with an objective, results that are recorded, promises that are followed up.</li>
</ol>

## Strategy: the one-to-one approach

The sales force strategy rests on a **one-to-one approach**: for every important customer, a separate situation analysis, a separate objective and a separate action plan. Catalogues and promotions carry the same message to everyone; the sales rep is the channel that makes the message specific to the customer.

<div class="grid2">
<div class="box"><h4>Mass communication</h4><p>Catalogues, promotions, social media. The same message to a broad audience. Low cost, low personalisation.</p></div>
<div class="box dark"><h4>One-to-one selling</h4><p>A proposal built on the customer’s situation. High cost, high impact. That is why it must be spent on the right customers.</p></div>
</div>

## Two measures of success: effectiveness and efficiency

| | Effectiveness | Efficiency |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Are we doing the right things? | Are we doing things right? |
| Focus | Right customer, right objective, right offer | Visit count, time, cost, conversion |
| Typical indicator | Share growth in target customers, customer profitability | Quality visits per day, sales per visit, cost of sales / sales |
| Risk | Being efficient with the wrong customers | Visiting the right customers unprepared |

<div class="callout"><span class="t">Manager’s note</span><p>Effectiveness first, then efficiency. Visiting the wrong portfolio faster only accelerates the problem.</p></div>

## How to use this guide

The guide has 11 modules and should be read as a chain: **customer selection → targets → performance monitoring → new customers → visits → organisation → profiles → pay and incentives → training and coaching → compliance**. Each module explains the management logic, then implementation steps, common mistakes and check questions for managers. The tools next to each module can be used directly in the field.

## Manager check questions

<ul class="check">
<li>Does my team define its role as “taking orders” or “growing the customer’s business”?</li>
<li>Does every rep know the potential of the top 10 customers in their portfolio?</li>
<li>Do we measure success only by sales, or also by share, profitability and behaviour quality?</li>
<li>When a rep leaves, does customer knowledge stay in the system?</li>
<li>Do we track effectiveness and efficiency indicators separately?</li>
</ul>
