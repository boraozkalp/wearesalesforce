## Why organisation matters

The same people can produce very different results in a different structure. In a team with unclear roles, good reps become managers, managers become report-chasers, and reports become files nobody reads. A clear organisation gives customers one face, clear decision rights, measurable accountability and a career path.

## Core principles

<div class="grid2">
<div class="box"><h4>Customer-centred</h4><p>The structure follows customer segments and channels, not internal department boundaries.</p></div>
<div class="box"><h4>Manageable span</h4><p>6–10 reps per sales force manager. More makes coaching impossible.</p></div>
<div class="box"><h4>Clear decision rights</h4><p>Who decides on price, discount, payment terms and portfolio changes is written down.</p></div>
<div class="box"><h4>Management close to the field</h4><p>Managers spend at least 50% of their time in the field with their teams.</p></div>
</div>

| Role | Main responsibility | Measure of success |
|---|---|---|
| Head of Sales Force | Strategy, budget, organisation, alignment with other departments | Commercial targets, cost-of-sales ratio, team capability |
| Regional Manager | Regional targets, coaching managers, resource allocation | Regional results, manager development |
| Sales Force Manager / Team Lead | Day-to-day team management, field coaching, portfolio balance | Team results, coaching frequency and quality |
| Account Manager (farmer) | Growing portfolio customers | Share of wallet, penetration, profitability |
| New Business Specialist (hunter) | Winning new customers | Customers placing a second order and active after 90 days |
| Key Account Manager | Chains and large multi-site customers | Annual agreements, profitability, service level |
| Sales Ops / CRM | Data quality, reporting, planning tools | Data accuracy, timely reports |
| Category / product support | Product knowledge, category arguments, training | Category penetration, training impact |

## Interfaces with other departments

- **Operations (warehouse, delivery, store):** delivery issues, stock-outs, special orders. A short weekly coordination meeting is recommended.
- **Finance:** credit limits, payment terms, overdue receivables. Reps are also responsible for collections; finance flags risky customers in time.
- **Marketing and category:** promotion calendar, pricing, new products. Field insights (competitor prices, customer demand) should flow back regularly.
- **HR:** recruitment, training, appraisal and incentive processes.

## Decision rights

| Decision | Rep | Sales force manager | Regional manager | Head |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Discount outside standard price | Within band | Above band | Exceptions | Policy |
| Change of payment terms | Propose | Approve (within limit) | Approve | Policy |
| Portfolio change | Propose | Approve | Informed | – |
| New customer acceptance (risk) | Propose | Approve | Approve if risky | – |

## Structure by company stage

- **Start-up / small:** one team; the manager also sells; hunting and farming are time blocks rather than roles.
- **Growth:** separate hunter and farmer roles, add CRM and reporting, set up a key account line.
- **Maturity:** teams by segment or channel (e.g. hotels, restaurants, chains), regional structure and specialist support roles.

## Talent and succession

Every manager position should have at least one “ready now” or “ready in 1–2 years” successor. Promoting a good salesperson without preparation means losing a good salesperson and gaining a weak manager. Four criteria help: performance, potential, alignment with company values and mobility.

## Common mistakes

1. Unclear role definitions. 2. Mixing hunter and farmer roles. 3. Disconnecting sales from operations. 4. Turning regional managers into report-chasers. 5. Underestimating CRM and reporting. 6. Leaving large customers with standard reps. 7. Category teams detached from the field. 8. Vague decision rights. 9. Promoting good salespeople without preparation. 10. Designing the organisation on paper without managing behaviour.

## Manager check questions

<ul class="check">
<li>Does our sales force managers’ span of control allow them to coach?</li>
<li>Are discount and payment-term authorities written down?</li>
<li>Do we have regular coordination with operations and finance?</li>
<li>Is there a successor for every manager position?</li>
</ul>
