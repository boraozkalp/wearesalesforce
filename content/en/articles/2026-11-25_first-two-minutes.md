The outcome of a sales visit is often decided at the opening, not the close. In the first two minutes, without realising it, the customer looks for answers to two questions:

1. Is this person worth my time?
2. What do they want from me?

If both answers are unclear, the customer stays on the defensive for the rest of the conversation.

## Four parts of a good opening

**1. A personal first sentence.** “How are you, how’s business?” is what everyone says. Start with an observation from your preparation: “You’ve opened the terrace; how are weekends going?” It shows interest and starts the discovery.

**2. State the purpose.** “Today I’d like to show you how we could solve the weekend stock problem in your breakfast service.” The customer shouldn’t have to guess why you’re there.

**3. State the time.** “It takes about 15 minutes.” Giving a time makes it easier for a busy owner to say yes, and keep to it.

**4. Get agreement.** “Does that work, or would another time be better?” This small question turns the meeting from your presentation into a shared decision.

## Avoid

- Opening by putting the catalogue on the table
- Mentioning price or promotions in the first sentence
- Missing how busy the customer is right now
- Starting by criticising a competitor

## A small exercise

For your next five visits, write your opening sentence in advance. After each visit, ask one question: “Did the customer understand my purpose in the first two minutes?” After five visits you’ll see your opening change.
