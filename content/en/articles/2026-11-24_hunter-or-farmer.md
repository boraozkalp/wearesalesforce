One of the most common complaints in sales teams is: “We’re not getting new customers.” Look closer and the picture is usually the same: reps are so busy with existing customers’ daily needs that there is no time left for new ones.

## Two different jobs

**Hunters** open new doors. They are used to rejection, build rapport quickly and enjoy uncertainty. Their success is measured not by the number of new accounts but by customers who **place a second order and are still buying three months later**.

**Farmers** grow existing customers. They analyse, patiently expand categories and learn the customer’s business. Their success is measured by share of wallet, penetration and profitability.

## When to split?

- **Small team (1–4 reps):** don’t split roles, split time. For example, each rep spends one day a week only on new customers, with a separate target.
- **Medium team (5–15 reps):** one or two people can be full-time hunters.
- **Large team:** a separate hunter team, with its own manager and a defined handover process.

## What happens without a handover?

The hunter wins the customer and runs to the next one; the farmer says “not my customer” and doesn’t engage. The customer gets lost in between. A written rule prevents this: for example, a customer with **at least 4 orders in at least 2 categories within 90 days** is handed to a farmer through a joint introduction visit, and the hunter earns a small share of that customer’s performance for three months after handover.

## A word on incentives

If you reward hunters only on “accounts opened”, you’ll open accounts that never buy. Base the main KPI on second orders and 90-day activity.
