New customer acquisition and canvassing

New customers don’t arrive by chance. A measurable acquisition process from defining the target customer to the second order.

New customer funnel

Why manage it separately?

Growing existing customers and winning new ones run at different rhythms. Existing customers are today’s sales and always feel urgent; new customers are tomorrow’s sales and keep getting postponed. Acquisition only becomes systematic with its own target, its own time block and, ideally, its own team (hunters).

Stages of the acquisition process

1. Define the target customer

Don’t go into the field without writing down who you want to win: customer type (independent restaurant, hotel, café chain, shop), size indicator (seats, rooms, floor space), location and fit with your delivery and payment model. A business that doesn’t fit is not a first-wave target, however attractive it looks.

2. Area and source analysis

Which neighbourhoods have a high density of businesses matching the profile? Who is around your existing customers? Sources: digital maps, industry directories, chamber of commerce records, social media, referrals from existing customers, supplier and distributor networks.

3. Prospect list

For each prospect at least: name, type, address, size indicator, decision-maker (if known), estimated potential, priority score. Update weekly.

4. Field canvassing

A systematic round in the chosen area: observe the business, introduce yourself briefly, find out who decides and when is best to visit. The aim is not to sell but to produce qualified prospects.

Canvassing observation list
  • How busy the business is and its customer profile
  • Menu / range and price level
  • Visible supplier vehicles and packaging
  • Decision-maker and best visiting time
  • Storage space and delivery conditions

5. Phone pre-contact

Book appointments with qualified prospects: short, polite, clear. “I’d like to show you in 15 minutes how we can help with X. Is Tuesday morning or Thursday afternoon better?”

6. First visit and offer

The first visit is a discovery visit, not a presentation: learn how the business works, what it needs, how satisfied it is with its current supplier and where it is frustrated. The offer should be based on this and kept narrow (2–3 categories).

7. Trial order

Offer an easy, low-risk way to try: limited categories, a clear delivery commitment, the rep following up the first delivery.

8. Convert to the second order

The first order is placed out of curiosity; the second out of satisfaction. Call or drop by within 48 hours of the first delivery: did product, delivery and invoice meet expectations? Fix any problem immediately.

New customer care

The first 90 days decide whether a customer stays. Keep visit frequency as high as an A customer, follow every order, and involve the manager if there is a problem. After 90 days the customer moves to a farmer’s portfolio against defined criteria (e.g. at least 4 orders in at least 2 categories).

New customer objections

Objection Likely reason Approach
“I’m happy with my supplier.” Risk of change, habit Respect it; offer to be a second source in one category
“Your prices are high.” Comparing unit price, not total cost Show total cost through waste, yield, delivery frequency and quality
“I have no time.” Doesn’t see the value of the visit Offer a clear 10-minute agenda and a concrete benefit; ask for the best time
“Do you deliver?” Operational concern State the conditions clearly; don’t promise what you can’t do

Daily reporting

At the end of each day: businesses canvassed, qualified prospects, appointments booked, first visits, offers made, trial orders. These six numbers show where the funnel is blocked straight away, not weeks later.

Tool

Work back from your target with the New customer funnel to see how many businesses you need to canvass.

New customer KPIs

  • New customers placing a second order (main KPI)
  • Share still active after 90 days
  • Average monthly sales and number of categories per new customer
  • Conversion rates at each stage of the funnel
  • Collection performance of new customers

Common mistakes

  1. Going into the field without a target customer definition. 2. Leaving a brochure and walking out. 3. Treating the first order as success. 4. Criticising the current supplier. 5. Not recording prospect information. 6. Spending time on low-potential businesses. 7. Sending the team out untrained. 8. Not writing handover rules. 9. Setting KPIs only on “number of accounts opened”. 10. Not checking delivery and payment fit at the start.

Manager check questions

  • Do we have a written target customer definition?
  • Is the new customer target based on the second order or 90-day activity?
  • At which stage of the funnel do we lose the most?
  • Is every new customer followed up within 48 hours of the first delivery?
