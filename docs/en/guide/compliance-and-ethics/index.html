Compliance, ethics and field conduct

What are our limits on the way to the target? Practical field rules for competition law, anti-corruption, personal data, conflicts of interest and expense integrity.

Why is this a sales force topic?

The sales team has more contact with the outside world than any other part of the company: customers, competitors’ staff, suppliers and sometimes public officials, every day. A single inappropriate conversation, email or gift can bring heavy fines and reputational damage to the company, and personal liability to the employee.

Important

This module offers a general framework and is not legal advice. Follow the laws in your country (e.g. competition, data protection, anti-bribery) and your company’s own policies; when in doubt, ask your legal or compliance team.

Compliance process (suggested)

  1. Every sales force employee receives a written briefing on their compliance responsibilities.
  2. The employee reads and signs it; the signed copy is kept in their personnel file.
  3. New hires sign it together with their employment contract.
  4. Any change is made by the compliance officer and re-signed by the whole team.
  5. A short compliance briefing is held at least twice a year and recorded.

Competition law: dos and don’ts in the field

Competition rules apply to every dealing with competitors, customers and suppliers. The core principle: every company sets its prices and terms independently.

Don’t You may
With competitors Discuss prices, discounts, promotions or customer allocation; make “gentlemen’s agreements” Chat about non-business topics at a trade fair or social event
Competitor prices Exchange price information with a competitor Monitor competitor prices via store visits, the internet or invoices customers show you, and set your own price independently
With suppliers Agree fixed or minimum resale prices; ask a supplier to influence other resellers Apply a recommended retail price by your own independent decision
With customers Tell a customer at what price to resell; pass price information between customers Help a customer calculate prices, clearly labelled as “recommended retail price”
Territories and customers Split territories or customers with a competitor Decide your own portfolio by your own strategy
Written communication Phrases like “let’s teach the competitor a lesson” or “destroy after reading”; destroying documents outside normal processes Write everything as if it could appear on a newspaper’s front page
Golden rule

The moment you realise a conversation with a competitor has turned to prices, discounts, promotions, customers or territories, end it clearly and report it to your legal team.

Anti-corruption

  • Never offer, promise or give a business partner any benefit (money, gifts, hospitality, business opportunities) in exchange for an unfair advantage.
  • Likewise, never request or accept such a benefit for yourself or anyone else.
  • Give no benefit to public officials in connection with their duties; report any such request to your manager and the compliance officer.
  • Gifts and hospitality must stay within company policy limits, be transparent and recorded. No entertaining in inappropriate venues or settings that could make participants uncomfortable.

Personal data and customer information

  • Collect and use customer and personal data only for business purposes and as far as the law allows.
  • Keep data in company systems; don’t circulate lists on personal devices, personal email or messaging groups.
  • Avoid emailing confidential documents; use authorised channels where needed.
  • Don’t take customer lists or company information with you when you leave; confidentiality continues after employment ends.

Conflicts of interest

Report to your manager when your personal interest conflicts with the company’s (e.g. a relative is a customer or supplier, a stake in a customer, working for a competitor). Use only objective criteria in commercial decisions and avoid even the appearance of a conflict.

Company assets, communication and expenses

  • Cars, phones, computers and other company assets are for business use; no private use without company approval.
  • Company communication systems must not be used for inappropriate, discriminatory or violent content, or content that damages the company’s reputation.
  • Time and expense reports are submitted on time, accurately and completely; only business expenses within policy are claimed.
  • Compliance with the law takes precedence over a manager’s conflicting instruction.

Respect and safety

Discrimination and any form of harassment are prohibited, in every relationship including employees, managers and customers. Follow health and safety rules in the field (driving, warehouse and kitchen environments).

How to report non-compliant behaviour

Reporting hotlines usually allow anonymous reports, but identifying yourself speeds up investigations. Employees who report in good faith must be protected.

Manager check questions

  • Has everyone in my team read and signed the compliance briefing?
  • Have we held a compliance briefing in the last six months?
  • Does my team know how to collect competitor price information correctly?
  • Are our gift and hospitality rules written down and known?
