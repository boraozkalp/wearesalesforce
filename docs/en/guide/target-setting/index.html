Target setting and KPI management

“Sell more” is not a target. How quantitative and qualitative targets are built, how a company target is broken down to customer level, and the SMART logic.

Without targets, a team only moves

Targets serve three audiences at once:

The company

Turns strategy into numbers and makes resource allocation and budgeting possible.

The team

Clarifies priorities, makes success visible and provides a fair basis for incentives.

The customer

Creates a customer-specific plan; visits stop being aimless chats.

Two families: quantitative and qualitative

Quantitative (result) targets Qualitative (behaviour and competency) targets
Sales Customer knowledge (potential, decision-maker, competitor)
Gross profit / margin Product and category knowledge
Active customers Quality of visit preparation
Purchase frequency Selling skills (questions, persuasion, objections, closing)
Average order value Reporting and data quality
Category penetration Internal communication and cooperation
New customers and reactivation Coaching evaluation score
Collections and overdue receivables
Balance rule

Quantitative targets measure “how much”, qualitative targets measure “how”. Focus only on sales and you lose quality; focus only on behaviour and you lose profitability.

SMART: one sentence per target

  • S – Specific: one customer group, one metric.
  • M – Measurable: the baseline is in the system.
  • A – Achievable: consistent with portfolio potential and past trend.
  • R – Relevant: serves strategy and profitability.
  • T – Time-bound: an end date and interim checkpoints.

Weak target: “Increase sales this month and be active in the field.” SMART target: “By the end of June I will grow frozen category sales in the A-segment restaurant portfolio by 12%, by trialling the category with 15 customers and without letting gross margin fall below 18%.”

The guardrail in the last clause is deliberate: it stops the target from being “bought” with discounts.

Breaking targets down: from desk to field

  1. Top-down: the company target is allocated to regions and teams in proportion to potential and capacity.
  2. Bottom-up reality check: reps and managers go through the portfolio customer by customer and estimate where growth is possible.
  3. Reconciliation: the gap between the two numbers is discussed. If it is large, either the target or the resources (portfolio, promotions, headcount) change.
  4. Customer-level targets: for each A and B customer, write which category should grow by how much and through which action.

Prerequisites

  • Clean customer data: you cannot allocate targets to closed or duplicate customers.
  • Category knowledge: which customer types use which categories and in what volume.
  • Competitor and market knowledge: is the market growing, is a competitor cutting prices?
  • Selling capability: does the team have the skills to deliver the target?

Sample KPI set

Area KPI Frequency
Commercial results Sales, gross profit, average order value Weekly / monthly
Customer development Active customers, penetration, share of wallet, lost customers Monthly
New customers New customers placing a second order, 90-day activity Monthly
Financial health Collection rate, overdue receivables Weekly
Behaviour Planned vs actual visits, visit report quality, coaching score Weekly / monthly

Targets and incentives

Targets are the basis of the incentive scheme, but not every target should be tied to the bonus. The more KPIs in the bonus, the weaker each becomes. Rule of thumb: at most four KPIs, each weighted at least 15%. See Module 9.

Common mistakes

  • Adding a fixed percentage to last year and calling it a target.
  • Giving everyone the same uplift regardless of portfolio potential.
  • Setting only a sales target without tracking margin and collections.
  • Setting targets at the start of the year with no interim reviews.

Manager check questions

  • Can every rep explain their target by customer and category?
  • Do targets include at least one qualitative (behaviour) element?
  • Is there a guardrail that prevents hitting the target through discounts?
  • Has the gap between top-down and bottom-up numbers been discussed?
